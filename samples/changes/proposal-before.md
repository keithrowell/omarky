# Proposal: Claims handling review for Harbourline Mutual

Prepared for the Chief Operating Officer, Harbourline Mutual. Draft for discussion.

## Situation

Harbourline Mutual handles around 40,000 claims a year across home, contents and motor. Claims staff work in two systems: the policy platform that holds the member record, and a claims workbench that was built in-house eight years ago. Most claims move between the two by hand, and the team keeps its own spreadsheets to track where each claim is up to.

The claims team has grown from 22 to 35 people in three years while claim volumes have risen by a quarter. Average time to settle a straightforward home claim is now 19 days, against a target of 10. Member complaints about claim delays have doubled over the same period and are the largest single category in the complaints register.

The executive wants a clear view of where time goes in the claims process, which steps could be automated or supported with AI, and what it would take to bring settlement times back inside target without adding more staff.

## Approach

We propose a twelve-week engagement in three stages. The first stage maps the claims process as the team works it, not as the manual describes it, by sitting with claims handlers and tracing real claims from lodgement to settlement. The second stage assesses each step for its suitability for automation or AI support, using our process scorecard, and builds the business case for the highest-value changes. The third stage designs the target process and a roadmap for getting there, with the first two changes specified in enough detail to start building.

A steering group of the Chief Operating Officer, the Head of Claims and the Head of Technology meets fortnightly through the engagement. We bring findings to the steering group as we have them rather than holding them for the final report, so that the organisation can act on the quick wins while the rest of the work continues.

The stages are:

- Stage 1, weeks 1 to 4: process mapping and time study across home, contents and motor claims
- Stage 2, weeks 5 to 8: opportunity assessment and business case for the top five changes
- Stage 3, weeks 9 to 12: target process design, roadmap and specifications for the first two changes

An optional fourth stage would see us stay on to oversee the build of the first two changes with the organisation's own technology team. We have not priced that stage here; it would be scoped at the end of Stage 3 once the changes are known.

## Team

Two principal consultants lead the engagement and do the work themselves. One leads the process and operations side, working with the claims team and the Head of Claims. The other leads the technology and AI side, working with the Head of Technology on what the current systems can support and what each change would need.

Both have delivered process reviews and AI opportunity assessments for member-owned organisations in insurance and superannuation. We do not use junior staff or offshore teams.
